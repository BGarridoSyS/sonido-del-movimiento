The work

A country turned into an instrument.

The aim of the exhibition is to show the cultural contribution of seismology: from the native peoples' account of the creation of the world and the interaction between the gods and humankind (the Mapuche worldview) to present-day science, as a tool for preserving and documenting historical milestones. It mixes visual techniques, such as projection mapping and 3D printing, with different musical techniques and styles.

The room

Four panels. One relator.

The exhibition takes place in a single room. Two pairs of panels surround the relator instrument, the relator being the 3D-printed relief that tells the story: those on the east and west walls show a cartoon-style animation, and those on the north and south walls, the transcript of the narration. The relator is a 3D print of Chile (17°S–43°S), resting on the subduction of the Nazca plate beneath the South American plate, where most of the country's earthquakes occur. It is made up of 26 sections of one degree of latitude each. A projection mapping that tells the same story as the panels is cast onto it.

The moments advance in chronological order, following the advances of seismology, from belief to a scientific understanding of earthquakes, supported by excerpts from scientific articles. The music travels through time in the opposite direction: it begins with artificial intelligence and ends with natural sounds.

The audience is part of the work. In moment 0 they assemble the relator with their own hands, section by section, and then surround it on both sides to listen.

From the author

A new instrument

“The exhibition is inspired by the creation of a new musical instrument, capable of playing the seismic cycle.”

The sound of movement is an invitation to a journey through space and time across one of the most seismic zones in the world: the subduction of the Nazca plate beneath the South American plate. The journey begins with the story of Tren Tren and Caicai Vilu and extends to the coronavirus pandemic, showing the interrelation between culture and seismology.

The exhibition is inspired by the creation of a new musical instrument, capable of playing the seismic cycle, and by the work in seismology of Professor Sergio Ruiz.

This experience has allowed me to delve deeper into science and technology as well as into different artistic expressions. In this process, I highlight the legacy and impact of professors Rodolfo Saragoni, Raúl Madariaga and José Vicente Asuar, as well as of the musicians and poets Violeta Parra, Nicanor Parra and Jorge Peña Hen.

Finally, I would like to thank everyone who has devoted time to this project and enriched it.

— Bastián I. Garrido Kogan · presentation text of the exhibition

Build the work

3D printing kit · five arches, 26 sections

Build the work at your school.

Instead of transporting the work, it can be printed where it is going to be shown. The relator is assembled by arches: each arch bears one word of the title, El · Sonido · Del · Movimiento · 2026, rests on its columns and holds, with tubes, its sections of one degree of latitude: on top, the relief of continental Chile; below, the Nazca plate subducting beneath the South American plate.

Printing and assembling the relator in the classroom repeats moment 0 of the exhibition, The creation: each group prints an arch and each student places their piece, from north to south, until the country is complete.

How to print

  1. Each arch has four files: columns, arch, tubes and sections. The parts come already oriented and laid out on the bed; in the slicer they can be rearranged or split across several prints.
  2. PLA, 0.2 mm layer height, 3 perimeters, 4 top and bottom layers and 15% infill. No supports; the tubes print standing, with a brim.
  3. For another scale, scale all parts equally: 200% for 1:2 and 400% for 1:1.
  4. When removing each section, write its number on the underside (the north cut face).

How to assemble it

  1. Columns standing, with the wide base on the floor. The top peg fits into the slot in the bottom of the arch.
  2. Arch with the word readable from the west; the tube holes face up.
  3. Tubes: one end in the arch, the other in the hole under each section. Then the sections from north to south, 1 to 26, with the cut faces touching and the trench facing west.
  4. The five arches line up from north to south, and the walkthrough videos tell the story over the assembled relator.

Joints with 0.2 mm clearance (column peg 5.5 mm into the arch; Ø 5 mm tubes, 7.5 mm into the arch and into the section). Filament and time are indicative: PLA (1.24 g/cm³) with 3 perimeters and 15% infill, at a rate of 50 g every 90 min and CLP 13,000 per kilogram. The dimensions given are those of the set of parts as it comes in each file.

Model dimensions

Those marked “model V5” come from the digital installation: lens heights, distances to walls, image sizes and throw ratios, verified by ray tracing (no shadows from the audience on the panels or the relator). The 8.3 × 6.0 × 4.0 m room is assumed: with another floor plan, the positions are recalculated.

Before purchasing

Validate at the venue the clear height, the ceiling anchor points, the electrical capacity and the blackout. Confirm with the supplier that the overhead projector allows vertical mounting and that the panel projectors cover the image at a 0.50 throw ratio.

Content

Three video signals: relator mapping, cartoon (E and W) and transcript (N and S), plus stereo audio. The projections onto the relator come pre-corrected for the relief; at the venue only the position is adjusted, with no keystone correction.
